# Hosting, DOI releases and reuse rights

[Documentation home](README.md) • Provider and policy check: 9 September 2026

## Provider-neutral by design

A PolyClank workbench does not need a DOI or an academic email to participate in the proposed protocol. It needs an accessible selected public record, readable documentation, exact artifact references, and clear reuse information. A DOI is a useful additional release identifier, not a verification badge and not permission to mirror a deposit elsewhere.

Separate the living workbench from frozen releases. A release records the exact included artifacts and can be mirrored only where both rights and provider rules permit. Keep version-specific identifiers distinct from collection identifiers. Hosting credentials and private cloud links never go into the public peer directory.

## Zenodo: registration and verification are different questions

Zenodo's current signup page asks for an institutional email. Its anti-spam guidance encourages institutional addresses, and its verification FAQ says institutional affiliation is a criterion for verified users. Its account guide nevertheless documents GitHub, ORCID and local signup. These sources establish institutional preferences and verification rules; they do not, by themselves, establish that every personal-email applicant is prohibited from creating an account. Actual moderation outcomes may differ. No signup attempt was made here. [Z1–Z4]

Use truthful account information and official support where an independent researcher is blocked. Do not buy, forge or borrow an apparent affiliation to circumvent verification. Choose another permitted host rather than making eligibility evasion part of PolyClank.

## Figshare: useful, but not an unrestricted mirror

Figshare documents DOI assignment for publicly published research outputs. Its free-platform policy is narrower than a general-purpose file host: it lists existing-DOI content, copyrighted content and content the uploader does not own among restrictions, and specifically warns against uploading others' journal articles. Therefore do not assume a permission to redistribute elsewhere automatically makes an item suitable for free figshare.com. Ask the provider about a particular mixed or collectively authored deposit. [F1, F2]

The relevant distinction is not that only personally invented mathematics can be discussed. It is whether a specific deposit is permitted by applicable rights and the service's submission rules. A new calculation or exposition can cite prior work without uploading the cited paper. The free and institutionally configured Figshare services should not be treated as one identical policy surface.

## Other routes and their limits

OSF documents DOI-bearing public registrations. These can serve as dated research records, not necessarily preregistrations. However, its current transition notice says new Projects and Components stop on 16 November 2026 and existing Projects become read-only on 19 February 2027, while Registrations and Preprints continue. Do not design PolyClank around OSF as a permanent mutable workspace. Check registration suitability, moderation and storage before depositing. [O1, O2]

The main viXra site currently directs AI-assisted submissions to ai.viXra. That service has its own conditions: PDF submissions, disclosure and responsibility requirements, and restrictions including pseudonyms and unjustified affiliation. Its documentation does not make it a generic workbench transport or an established DOI route for this project. [V1, V2]

A static website, another Git host, or an explicitly shared cloud folder may be sufficient for living work. Whether it is easy to fetch anonymously, version exactly and keep available must be tested. Private material in the same account must remain outside the published manifest. A volunteer mirror needs an explicit selection and rights review, not permission to copy the entire account.

## AI assistance does not automatically clear every file for redistribution

In the United States, the Copyright Office says sufficient human-determined expression can be protected, while merely providing prompts is insufficient. Using AI as an aid does not disqualify a larger human-authored work. Its statement does not make every AI-assisted repository public domain. [C1]

Jurisdictions differ. The UK government's March 2026 report discusses existing computer-generated-work provisions, alongside policy proposals. A proposal is not an enacted repeal. This is another reason not to make a global legal assumption from a US summary. [C2]

Mathematical discoveries and the original expression of an exposition are separate issues. A generated file can contain third-party expression or human contributions. Citation is not a replacement for reuse permission. Authorship credit, provenance, ownership, permission to publish, and permission to use a service are distinct fields.

The operational rule is item-specific review: publish material the operator is authorized to publish, identify any applicable license or public-domain basis, retain third-party notices, and omit or link to material whose redistribution is not established. Apply licensing or dedication only to rights that the contributor can actually grant. No automatic upload of local literature or unreviewed transcripts.

A public library index may identify a book or theorem without publishing the book. Independent exposition, permitted translations, new proofs and formalizations remain useful programmes. They should preserve intellectual attribution and avoid reproducing protected text or an author's distinctive exposition under the label of AI paraphrase.

These are design safeguards and a dated source summary, not a legal opinion on any participant's particular collection.
