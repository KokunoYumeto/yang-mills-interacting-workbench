# Degree-two gradient bounds and the complete original bulk-link sum

This note proves a score-uniform Dirichlet inequality for the complete
fourteen-dimensional space of quaternion polynomials of degree at most two.
The affine part remains present throughout the proof. The estimate is then
applied to the original raw function \(F=p-r\), with every original word,
coefficient, physical face and vacuum retained. The general exponent is six,
and it is optimal. Summing distinct physical derivatives gives an explicit
original-path energy lower bound of order \(j^{-30}\). The sign of the
shrinking-window expression does not follow from this lower bound.

## 1. Group, metric and the full polynomial space

Put \(T_\alpha=-i\sigma_\alpha/2\), and write
\[
 U=c(U)I+2\sum_{\alpha=1}^3x_\alpha(U)T_\alpha,\qquad
 c(U)^2+|x(U)|^2=1,\qquad q(U)=2x(U).
 \tag{PG1}
\]
Haar measure \(dU\) is probability measure. The left fields generated by
the \(T_\alpha\) are orthonormal. Thus the metric is the round metric of
radius two, not the radius-one metric of the displayed coordinates.
Let \(\mathcal H_k\) be the restrictions of homogeneous harmonic
polynomials of degree \(k\) in the four real coordinates \((c,x)\).
We use every coefficient of
\(P\in\mathcal H_0\oplus\mathcal H_1\oplus\mathcal H_2\);
complex coefficients are allowed. The projection onto \(\mathcal H_2\)
is denoted by \(\Pi_2\).

For a cap centred at the identity, use
\(c=\cos\theta\), \(x=\sin\theta\,\omega\), \(\omega\in S^2\).
Its physical radius is \(r\), its polar-angle radius is \(R=r/2\), and
we shall use \(0<r\le1\). Every complete polynomial has a unique form
\[
 \begin{split}
 P={}&A+B_{\rm aff} c+C c^2+\sin\theta\,(b+c\,d)\cdot\omega
           +\sin^2\theta\,\omega^{\mathsf T}T\omega,\\
 &A,B_{\rm aff},C\in\mathbb C,\quad b,d\in\mathbb C^3,\quad
 T^{\mathsf T}=T,\quad\operatorname{tr}T=0 .
 \end{split}\tag{PG2}
\]
Indeed, split the spatial quadratic matrix into its trace and traceless
parts, and replace its trace term using \(|x|^2=1-c^2\). This accounts
for \(3+3+3+5=14\) coefficients, with no discarded constant or affine term.
The quadratic harmonic part and its exact full-Haar norm are
\[
 \Pi_2P=C(c^2-\tfrac14)+c\sin\theta\,d\cdot\omega
                   +\sin^2\theta\,\omega^{\mathsf T}T\omega,\qquad
 \|\Pi_2P\|_2^2=\frac{|C|^2}{16}
                  +\frac{|d|^2}{24}+\frac{\|T\|_{\rm HS}^2}{12}.
 \tag{PG3}
\]
To verify all constants, the four-coordinate Haar moments are
\(\int z_i z_j=\delta_{ij}/4\) and
\(\int z_i z_jz_kz_l=
(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}
+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})/24\).
Rotational invariance forces these forms; tracing once and twice,
using \(\sum z_i^2=1\), gives the denominators. They prove (PG3)
and the orthogonality of its three summands. They also prove directly
that all its displayed quadratics are harmonic in four variables.

Let \(d\omega\) be probability area measure on \(S^2\). The metric and
radial Haar formula are
\[
 |\nabla P|^2=\tfrac14\bigl(|\partial_\theta P|^2+
                \sin^{-2}\theta\,|\nabla_\omega P|^2\bigr),\qquad
 dU=\frac2\pi\sin^2\theta\,d\theta\,d\omega,\qquad
 v(r)=\frac{r-\sin r}{2\pi}.
 \tag{PG4}
\]
All expressions at \(\theta=0\) are interpreted by smooth continuation.

## 2. Exact cap Dirichlet matrices, including every affine coefficient

Define the following real integrals, all over \(0\le\theta\le R\):
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 J_0=\int\sin^2\theta\,d\theta,\qquad
 I_0=\int\sin^4\theta\,d\theta,\qquad
 I_1=\int\sin^4\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta,\qquad
 I_2=\int\sin^4\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta,\\
 V_0=\int\sin^2\theta(\cos^2\theta+2)\,d\theta,\\
 V_1=\int\sin^2\theta\cos\theta(2\cos^2\theta+1)\,d\theta,\\
 V_2=\int\sin^2\theta(4\cos^4\theta-2\cos^2\theta+1)\,d\theta .
 \end{gathered}\tag{PG5}
\]
Their exact endpoint evaluations are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_0&=R/2-\sin(2R)/4,\\
 I_0&=3R/8-\sin(2R)/4+\sin(4R)/32,\\
 I_1&=\sin^5R/5,\\
 I_2&=R/16-\sin(2R)/64-\sin(4R)/64+\sin(6R)/192,\\
 V_0&=9R/8-\sin(2R)/2-\sin(4R)/32,\\
 V_1&=\sin^3R-\tfrac25\sin^5R,\\
 V_2&=R/2-3\sin(2R)/16-\sin(6R)/48 .
 \end{aligned}\tag{PG6}
\]
Differentiating each right side gives its integrand in (PG5), and
each vanishes at zero; this proves every equality without an
asymptotic replacement.

The exact cap average
\(v(r)^{-1}\int_{d(I,U)\le r}|\nabla P|^2dU\)
is \(E_0+E_1+E_2\), where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_0&=\frac1{4J_0}
  \begin{pmatrix}\overline{B_{\rm aff}}&\overline C\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}I_0&2I_1\\2I_1&4I_2\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}B_{\rm aff}\\C\end{pmatrix},\\
 E_1&=\frac1{12J_0}\sum_{\alpha=1}^3
  \begin{pmatrix}\overline b_\alpha&\overline d_\alpha\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}V_0&V_1\\V_1&V_2\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}b_\alpha\\d_\alpha\end{pmatrix},\\
 E_2&=\frac{\|T\|_{\rm HS}^2}{30J_0}(4I_2+6I_0).
 \end{aligned}\tag{PG7}
\]
The row and column of the constant \(A\) are zero. No other row or
column has been omitted.

Here is a direct proof of the block decomposition. On \(S^2\),
\[
 \int|v\cdot\omega|^2d\omega=|v|^2/3,\qquad
 \int|\omega^{\mathsf T}T\omega|^2d\omega=2\|T\|_{\rm HS}^2/15 .
 \tag{PG8}
\]
The same rotational-moment argument as above proves both identities.
The angular Laplacian has eigenvalues \(0,-2,-6\) on these constant,
linear and traceless quadratic restrictions. This follows by writing
the Euclidean three-dimensional Laplacian in polar coordinates and
applying it to homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degrees zero, one
and two. Integration by parts therefore makes angular derivative
cross terms zero and multiplies their angular squared norms by
zero, two and six. Radial derivative cross terms vanish by angular
orthogonality. Finally,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \partial_\theta(B_{\rm aff}c+Cc^2)&=-\sin\theta(B_{\rm aff}+2C\cos\theta),\\
 \partial_\theta[\sin\theta(b+c\,d)]
            &=c\,b+(2c^2-1)d,\\
 \partial_\theta[\sin^2\theta\,T]&=2\sin\theta\,c\,T .
 \end{aligned}
\]
Substitution into (PG4), using (PG8), proves (PG7), including its
off-diagonal entries.

## 3. Explicit cap lower bounds of orders six, four and two

Set \(\alpha_0=23/24\). For \(0\le\theta\le R\le1/2\),
\[
 \alpha_0\theta\le\sin\theta\le\theta,\qquad
 J_0\le R^3/3,\qquad I_0\le R^5/5.
 \tag{PG9}
\]
The lower sine bound follows from
\(\sin\theta\ge\theta-\theta^3/6\).
The scalar block can be completed exactly:
\[
 E_0=\frac{I_0}{4J_0}|B_{\rm aff}+2C I_1/I_0|^2
       +\frac{|C|^2}{J_0}(I_2-I_1^2/I_0).
 \tag{PG10}
\]
In particular the estimate below holds for the actual affine
coefficient \(B_{\rm aff}\); optimization in this identity does not replace it.
The covariance determinant is
\[
 I_0I_2-I_1^2=
 \frac12\int_0^R\int_0^R
 \sin^4\theta\sin^4\phi(\cos\theta-\cos\phi)^2\,d\theta\,d\phi
 \ \ge\ \frac{\alpha_0^{10}R^{14}}{2205}.
 \tag{PG11}
\]
For the inequality, integrate (PG9) between \(\theta\) and \(\phi\)
to obtain
\(|\cos\theta-\cos\phi|\ge
\alpha_0|\theta^2-\phi^2|/2\).
The remaining double integral is exactly
\[
 \int_0^R\int_0^R\theta^4\phi^4(\theta^2-\phi^2)^2\,d\theta\,d\phi
 =2R^{14}\left(\frac1{45}-\frac1{49}\right)
 =\frac{8R^{14}}{2205}.
\]
Combining (PG9)–(PG11) proves
\[
 E_0\ge\frac{\alpha_0^{10}R^6}{147}|C|^2
       =\frac{\alpha_0^{10}}{588}\,r^6\,\frac{|C|^2}{16}.
 \tag{PG12}
\]

For the vector block the two exact squared channels are
\[
 E_1=\frac1{12J_0}\int_0^R\sin^2\theta
 \bigl(|c\,b+(2c^2-1)d|^2+2|b+c\,d|^2\bigr)d\theta .
 \tag{PG13}
\]
For each component and each \(\theta\), the Gram determinant of
the two vectors \((c,\sqrt2)\) and \((2c^2-1,\sqrt2c)\) is
\(2\sin^4\theta\). Completing the square in \(b_\alpha\) yields
the pointwise lower bound
\(2\sin^4\theta|d_\alpha|^2/(c^2+2)\).
This is at least \(2\sin^4\theta|d_\alpha|^2/3\).
Since \(\int_0^R\sin^6\theta\,d\theta\ge\alpha_0^6R^7/7\),
\[
 E_1\ge\frac{\alpha_0^6R^4}{42}|d|^2
       =\frac{\alpha_0^6}{28}\,r^4\,\frac{|d|^2}{24}.
 \tag{PG14}
\]
The tensor block in (PG7) retains both contributions. Its exact
integrand contains \(4c^2+6\ge6\), so
\[
 E_2\ge\frac{\|T\|_{\rm HS}^2I_0}{5J_0}
       \ge\frac{3\alpha_0^4R^2}{25}\|T\|_{\rm HS}^2
       =\frac{9\alpha_0^4}{25}\,r^2
                       \frac{\|T\|_{\rm HS}^2}{12}.
 \tag{PG15}
\]
Define the exact positive constant
\[
 c_6=\frac{(23/24)^{10}}{588}
     =\frac{41426511213649}{37281188007641088}
     >2^{-10}.
 \tag{PG16}
\]
The two other constants in (PG14)–(PG15) exceed \(c_6\).
Since \(r\le1\), (PG3), (PG12), (PG14) and (PG15) imply
\[
 \frac1{v(r)}\int_{d(z,U)\le r}|\nabla P(U)|^2dU
                  \ge c_6r^6\|\Pi_2P\|_2^2
 \quad\text{for every }z\in SU(2).
 \tag{PG17}
\]
To prove the passage to every centre, left multiplication by \(z\)
is a real orthogonal linear map of the four quaternion coordinates.
It preserves the radius-two metric, Haar measure, harmonic degree
and the norm of \(\Pi_2P\). Apply the identity-centred proof to
\(U\mapsto P(zU)\). Its affine coefficients can change, but (PG10)
and (PG13) hold for every value of those coefficients.

## 4. A unit density with bounded score

Let \(\rho\) be a positive \(C^1\) density with
\(\int\rho\,dU=1\) and \(|\nabla\log\rho|\le B\), \(B\ge0\),
in precisely the metric (PG4). The geodesic integral of its score
gives \(\rho(U)\ge e^{-Br}\rho(z)\) whenever \(d(U,z)\le r\).
Multiply (PG17) by \(\rho(z)\), and integrate in \(z\).
Fubini and the reverse estimate
\(\rho(z)\le e^{Br}\rho(U)\) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 c_6r^6\|\Pi_2P\|_2^2
 &\le\frac1{v(r)}\int|\nabla P(U)|^2
                      \int_{d(z,U)\le r}\rho(z)\,dz\,dU\\
 &\le e^{Br}\int|\nabla P(U)|^2\rho(U)\,dU .
 \end{split}\tag{PG18}
\]
The cap volume has cancelled exactly. Thus no dimension-three
volume penalty is required.

Choose \(r=2/(B+2)\), and put \(t=B/(B+2)\in[0,1)\).
Then \(r(1+B)=1+t\), \(Br=2t\), and
\(\log(1+t)\ge t/2\), by integrating
\((1+t)^{-1}\ge1/2\) over \([0,t]\). It follows that
\((1+t)^6e^{-2t}\ge1\). We have proved
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \int|\nabla P|^2\rho\,dU\ge
       \eta_6(B)\|\Pi_2P\|_2^2,\qquad
 \eta_6(B)=c_6(1+B)^{-6}.}
 \tag{PG19}
\]
The constant part has zero gradient; arbitrary affine terms are
still present on the left. No orthogonality under \(\rho\) was assumed.
Optimizing (PG18) over \(0<r\le1\) also proves
\[
 \int|\nabla P|^2\rho\,dU\ge\Lambda_6(B)\|\Pi_2P\|_2^2,\qquad
 \Lambda_6(B)=
 \begin{cases}c_6e^{-B},&0\le B\le6,\\
 c_6(6/(eB))^6,&B\ge6.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{PG20}
\]
For a finite vector of such polynomials, summing the proved
inequality component by component proves the same result.

## 5. The exponent six is necessary

The full polynomial \(P_*=(1-c)^2=1-2c+c^2\) has
\(\|\Pi_2P_*\|_2^2=1/16\). Its affine term is essential to its
third-order gradient zero at \(I\). For \(B\ge1\), retain
\[
 t(U)=\sqrt{8(1-c(U))},\quad
 f_B(U)=\sqrt{B^{-2}+t(U)^2},\quad
 \rho_B(U)=Z_B^{-1}e^{-Bf_B(U)},\quad
 Z_B=\int e^{-Bf_B(U)}dU .
 \tag{PG21}
\]
These are smooth positive densities because \(f_B\) is the square
root of a strictly positive smooth function. Direct differentiation
in the radius-two metric gives
\[
 |\nabla t|^2=1-t^2/16,\qquad
 |\nabla f_B|^2=
 \frac{t^2(1-t^2/16)}{B^{-2}+t^2}\le1,\qquad
 |\nabla P_*|^2=\frac{t^6}{256}-\frac{t^8}{4096}.
 \tag{PG22}
\]
The first equality is used away from \(I\) and continued in the
second equality; \(t\) itself need not be smooth at \(I\).
Thus \(|\nabla\log\rho_B|\le B\). The exact radial Haar measure is
\[
 dU=\frac{t^2}{4\pi}\sqrt{1-t^2/16}\,dt,\quad0\le t\le4,\qquad
 I_k=\int_0^\infty u^k e^{-\sqrt{1+u^2}}\,du .
 \tag{PG23}
\]
This follows from \(t=4\sin(\theta/2)\) and (PG4).
Substituting \(u=Bt\), the denominator \(4\pi B^3Z_B\) is
\(\int_0^{4B}u^2\sqrt{1-u^2/(16B^2)}
e^{-\sqrt{1+u^2}}du\), which tends to \(I_2>0\).
For the gradient numerator the same substitution and (PG22) give
\[
 B^6\int|\nabla P_*|^2\rho_BdU
 =\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^{4B}
  \left(\frac{u^8}{256}-\frac{u^{10}}{4096B^2}\right)
       \sqrt{1-\frac{u^2}{16B^2}}e^{-\sqrt{1+u^2}}du}
 {\displaystyle\int_0^{4B}u^2
       \sqrt{1-\frac{u^2}{16B^2}}e^{-\sqrt{1+u^2}}du}
 \longrightarrow \frac{I_8}{256I_2}>0 .
 \tag{PG24}
\]
Dominated convergence is justified by \(u^k e^{-u}\), for each
displayed power, after extending each integrand by zero past \(4B\).
The numerator can equivalently be written as
\(u^8(1-u^2/(16B^2))^{3/2}e^{-\sqrt{1+u^2}}/256\).
Consequently no positive constant times \((1+B)^{-k}\|\Pi_2P\|_2^2\)
can hold uniformly for \(k<6\). This proves optimality of the
exponent, not optimality of the numerical constant (PG16).

## 6. Complete original lattice and score receiving map

Let \(m=2L\), \(n=m+1\), \(h=\pi/(2n)\), with \(L\ge2\).
Vertices have indices \(0\le i,k,l\le m\) and physical positions
\(o+a(i-L,k-L,l-L)\). All positively oriented nearest-neighbour
edges and every elementary face of the open cube are retained.
The spanning tree consists of every direction-one edge, every
direction-two edge at first coordinate zero and every direction-three
edge at first two coordinates zero. Root paths run in directions
three, two, one from \((0,0,0)\). With ordered path transport \(t_s\),
the exact rooted word on a chord \(e=(s,t)\) is \(Z_e=t_sU_et_t^{-1}\).
The two chord sets and every original row coefficient are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \mathcal C_2=\{(i,k,l;2):1\le i\le m,\ 0\le k<m,\ 0\le l\le m\},\\
 \mathcal C_3=\{(i,k,l;3):0\le i,k\le m,\ (i,k)\ne(0,0),\
                                      0\le l<m\},\\
 v_0(t)=n^{-1/2},\quad
 v_1(t)=\sqrt{2/n}\cos\frac{\pi(t+1/2)}n,\quad
 w_1(t)=-\sqrt{2/n}\sin\frac{\pi(t+1)}n,\\
 a_{(i,k,l;2)}=-v_1(i)w_1(k)v_0(l)/\sqrt2,\quad
 a_{(i,k,l;3)}=0,\\
 b_{(i,k,l;2)}=v_0(i)w_1(k)v_1(l)/\sqrt2,\quad
 b_{(i,k,l;3)}=-v_0(i)v_1(k)w_1(l)/\sqrt2,\\
 u_1=\sum_{e\in\mathcal C_2\cup\mathcal C_3}a_eq(Z_e),\quad
 u_3=\sum_{e\in\mathcal C_2\cup\mathcal C_3}b_eq(Z_e),\\
 S=u_1\cdot u_3,\quad S_{\mathcal R}=S\circ\mathcal R,\quad
 \sigma_*=\sqrt8\sin h,\quad\beta_0=\frac{\sigma_*}{2g^2},\quad
 F=p-r=\beta_0(S-S_{\mathcal R}).
 \end{gathered}\tag{PG25}
\]
The symbol \(\mathcal R\) is the original direction-one graph
reflection, including its reflected root and every inverse edge
orientation. The polynomial coefficient \(B_{\rm aff}\) of Section 1 is
distinct from the score bound \(B\) and all boundary link indices.

The complete Hamiltonian and actual positive unit vacuum are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 H=\kappa H_0+b_{\rm mag}\sum_{\text{all faces }p}
                       (2-\operatorname{tr}U_p),\quad
 H_0=-\sum_{e,\alpha}X_{e,\alpha}^2,\quad H\psi=E_0\psi,\quad
 d\mu=\psi^2dU,\\
 \kappa=\frac{2g^2}{a},\quad b_{\rm mag}=\frac1{2g^2a},\quad
 \xi=\frac1{4g^4},\quad
 C_F=\langle F\psi,(H-E_0)F\psi\rangle
                      =\kappa\mu(|\nabla F|^2),\quad G=\mu(F^2).
 \end{gathered}\tag{PG26}
\]
The constant \(2b_{\rm mag}M\), \(M=3m^2(m+1)\), remains in the
Hamiltonian and vacuum equation. The displayed energy identity follows
by expanding \(H(F\psi)\), substituting \(H\psi=E_0\psi\), and
integrating the two derivative cross terms by parts on the compact
physical-link product.

The exact score statement read in the retained original TeX is
\[
 |\nabla_e\log\psi^2|\le4r_e\xi ,
 \tag{PG27}
\]
where \(r_e\) is the actual number of faces incident to the physical
edge. Its proof, including every mixed derivative, is in
[equations (77)–(79) of the original TeX](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/yang-mills-interacting-workbench/blob/485b61423c1f4e1805deb453c1b6b342b27842b6/yang-mills/continuations/20260930-s6-ns-moment-map-bridge/sources/spatial/spatial_continuum.tex#L1415)
and is repeated here to specify precisely what is used.
Writing \(u=\log\psi\), the vacuum equation implies
\(\Delta u+|\nabla u|^2=(V-E_0)/\kappa\).
The product Bochner formula gives
\[
 (\Delta+2\nabla u\cdot\nabla)|\nabla_eu|^2
 =2\sum_f|\nabla_f\nabla_eu|_{\rm HS}^2+
       |\nabla_eu|^2+2\xi\langle\nabla_eu,\nabla_eW\rangle ,
 \qquad W=\sum_p(2-\operatorname{tr}U_p).
\]
The coefficient one is twice the Ricci curvature \(1/2\) in the
radius-two metric. An incident face word is \(AUB\) or \(AU^{-1}B\);
bi-invariance reduces its trace gradient to that of \(2c\), whose
norm is at most one. Hence \(|\nabla_eW|\le r_e\). At a maximum
of \(|\nabla_eu|^2\), the Bochner identity gives
\(0\ge|\nabla_eu|^2-2\xi r_e|\nabla_eu|\).
This proves (PG27).

The tree-coordinate map sends physical product Haar to product Haar
in tree coordinates and all chords: solve successively
\(U_{(s,t)}=t_s^{-1}t_t\) for tree edges, and
\(U_e=t_s^{-1}Z_et_t\) for chords, using left and right Haar
invariance at each step. Gauge invariance of the positive vacuum
follows from uniqueness of the ground state and its positivity, so its
tree-coordinate density is independent of tree coordinates.
For a fixed chord and fixed tree coordinates, varying \(U_e\) varies
only \(Z_e=t_sU_et_t^{-1}\). Conjugation by \(t_s\) rotates the
three left derivative components orthogonally. Consequently the
physical squared derivative norm equals the chord squared derivative
norm, and the actual one-chord conditional score is bounded by
(PG27). The denominator in a conditional density is constant in that
chord variable. Integrating the density over other chords also preserves
the score bound: differentiate the integral and apply
\(|\nabla\rho|\le B\rho\). These facts justify both conditional and
marginal uses below without asserting independence.

## 7. Exact quadratic harmonic at every direction-two bulk chord

Take
\[
 e=(i,k,l;2),\quad1\le i\le m-1,\quad0\le k<m,\quad0\le l\le m,
 \qquad e_\partial(e)=(m,k,l;2).
 \tag{PG28}
\]
Write \(K=Z_e\), \(H_\partial=Z_{e_\partial(e)}\). Boundary links
are denoted by \(e_\partial\), while \(b_e\) always denotes the
original row coefficient in (PG25).

For completeness define the ordered boundary comb
\[
 A_{k,l}=
 \left(\prod_{t=0}^{l-1}Z_{(m,0,t;3)}\right)
 \left(\prod_{s=0}^{k-1}Z_{(m,s,l;2)}\right),\qquad A_{0,0}=I.
 \tag{PG29}
\]
Every product is in increasing displayed order, and an empty product
is identity. After evaluating the original root paths in tree gauge,
their reflected transports are \(A_{k,l}\), independently of the first
coordinate. Direction-one paths have identity transport in that gauge.
The unique reflected source containing the target chord \(K\) is
\((m-i,k,l;2)\), with the same edge orientation. Since
\(A_{k+1,l}=A_{k,l}H_\partial\), its entire reflected word is
\[
 Z^{\mathcal R}_{(m-i,k,l;2)}
       =A_{k,l}K H_\partial^{-1}A_{k,l}^{-1}.
 \tag{PG30}
\]
No other reflected target is \(K\), and \(K\) occurs in no boundary
comb because \(i<m\). The comb \(A_{k,l}\) is independent of
\(H_\partial\) as well: its direction-two factors end at \(k-1\).
The adjoint action of \(A_{k,l}\) on \(q\) is orthogonal, since
\(-2\operatorname{tr}(T_\alpha T_\beta)=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\).
It preserves both \(c\) and \(|q|^2\).

Let \(U_1,U_3\) be the complete original row sums excluding \(e\),
and \(V_1,V_3\) the complete reflected row sums excluding
\((m-i,k,l;2)\), respectively. All four depend on the exterior and
are independent of \(K\). Put \(Q=q(K)\),
\(Q'=\operatorname{Ad}_{A_{k,l}}q(KH_\partial^{-1})\).
The exact coefficient reflection is
\(a_{(m-i,k,l;2)}=-a_e\), \(b_{(m-i,k,l;2)}=b_e\), by
\(v_1(m-i)=-v_1(i)\). The full expansions, including constants, are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 S={}&U_1\cdot U_3+(b_eU_1+a_eU_3)\cdot Q
                              +a_eb_e|Q|^2,\\
 S_{\mathcal R}={}&V_1\cdot V_3+
                    (b_eV_1-a_eV_3)\cdot Q'-a_eb_e|Q'|^2,\\
 F={}&\beta_0\bigl[U_1\cdot U_3-V_1\cdot V_3
       +(b_eU_1+a_eU_3)\cdot Q
       -(b_eV_1-a_eV_3)\cdot Q'
       +a_eb_e(|Q|^2+|Q'|^2)\bigr].
 \end{aligned}\tag{PG31}
\]
Thus, for each exterior, \(F\) is in the complete space treated by
(PG19). Its remainder is affine in all four quaternion coordinates
of \(K\). Derivatives in the physical chord \(e\) differentiate
exactly this polynomial through the isometry already proved.

Set \(\chi_1(U)=4c(U)^2-1\), a degree-two harmonic of Haar squared
norm one. Since \(|q(U)|^2=3-\chi_1(U)\),
\[
 \Pi_2^{(e)}F=-\beta_0a_eb_e
          [\chi_1(K)+\chi_1(KH_\partial^{-1})],\qquad
 \|\Pi_2^{(e)}F\|_{L^2(dK)}^2
 =\frac43\beta_0^2a_e^2b_e^2[1+2c(H_\partial)^2].
 \tag{PG32}
\]
Indeed, multiplication by \(H_\partial^{-1}\) is an orthogonal
linear map of quaternion coordinates. If its scalar coordinate is
\(\ell\cdot z\), then \(|\ell|=1\) and
\(\ell\cdot(1,0,0,0)=c(H_\partial)\).
The fourth-moment identity preceding (PG4) gives
