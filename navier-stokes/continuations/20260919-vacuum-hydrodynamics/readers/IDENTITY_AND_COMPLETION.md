# The gravitational identity carried by Navier–Stokes strain

## 1. The identity and its finite-amplitude continuation

The identity obtained in the preceding interior calculation is

$$
\boxed{\mathfrak p^{[1]}_{ij}
=\frac{\delta\mathsf T^{\mathrm{TF}}_{ij}}{\varepsilon_c+p_c}
=-\frac{2\nu}{c^2}S_{ij},\qquad
S_{ij}=\frac{\partial_iU_j+\partial_jU_i}{2}.}
\tag{1.1}
$$

The superscript records an operation: first differentiate the metric with respect to its perturbation parameter at fixed interior radius, then take the interior endpoint limit of its shape operator. Equation (1.1) is the identity in that tensor response sector, with the frame, orientation, and enthalpy specified in the preserved manuscript (Interior continuation, §§4–6). It maps fluid strain into an anisotropic response of gravitational collapse data.

This continuation constructs a finite-amplitude family satisfying the exact five-dimensional Einstein equation. Let

$$
\mathcal B=-\frac{2\nu}{c^2}S,\qquad
\chi=\left(1+\frac43\operatorname{tr}\mathcal B^2\right)^{1/2},
$$

$$
P_w=\frac14-\frac{3}{4\chi},\qquad
P_\perp=\frac14I_3+\frac{I_3/4+\mathcal B}{\chi}.
\tag{1.2}
$$

The nonlinear identity is

$$
\boxed{
\chi\left(P_\perp-\frac14I_3\right)-\frac14I_3
=-\frac{2\nu}{c^2}S,
\qquad \chi(1-4P_w)=3.
}
\tag{1.3}
$$

Here $\chi$ is retained as the ratio of spatial volume densities of two explicitly marked Einstein metrics. Both metrics are compared using the same slice marking; the ratio is not asserted to be an absolute volume of an unmarked infinite homogeneous space. It is determined by the Hamiltonian constraint, not discarded after computing $P$. The resulting metric, its exact Einstein check, its finite-amplitude inverse, and its relationship to the full spatial field are given below.

Two constructions are carried out. A homogeneous Einstein family supplies the explicit nonlinear collapse map (1.3). A separate construction takes every smooth compactly supported divergence-free three-dimensional velocity field to exact four-dimensional gravitational constraint data, including its Laplacian contribution. Their common homogeneous specialization is computed. No prior claim of a global nonlinear fluid/gravity reconstruction or independent ER = EPR proof is used.

## 3. Exact nonlinear Einstein balance for anisotropy

Use Gaussian proper length $\lambda=cs$, where $s$ is proper time. The coordinate map $(s,y)\mapsto(\lambda=cs,y)$ pulls $-d\lambda^2+h$ back to $-c^2ds^2+h$. Write

$$
g=-d\lambda^2+h_{AB}(\lambda,y)dy^A dy^B,
\qquad A,B\in\{w,1,2,3\}.
$$

Define the mixed endomorphism and density

$$
B=\frac12h^{-1}\partial_\lambda h,\quad
\theta=\operatorname{tr}B,\quad
E=B-\frac\theta4I_4,\quad
V=\sqrt{\det h},\quad Q=VE.
\tag{3.1}
$$

Here $+\partial_\lambda$ points toward increasing remaining time. The future normal toward the singularity is $-\partial_\lambda$; its shape endomorphism is $K^\sharp=-B$. The sign is determined by this stated orientation.

Let $\mathcal R^A{}_B$ and $\mathcal R$ be the intrinsic Ricci endomorphism and scalar of $h$. Direct evaluation of the spatial Einstein equation gives

$$
\partial_\lambda B+\theta B
=-\mathcal R^A{}_B-\frac4{L^2}I_4.
\tag{3.2}
$$

Taking its tracefree part, and using $V'=\theta V$, proves the exact identity

$$
\boxed{
\partial_\lambda Q=-V\,\mathcal R^{\mathrm{TF}},\qquad
Q(\lambda)=Q(\lambda_*)-
\int_{\lambda_*}^{\lambda}V(u)\mathcal R^{\mathrm{TF}}(u)\,du.
}
\tag{3.3}
$$

The equality is in density-valued endomorphisms in the Gaussian transported chart. Equivalently, $VE\,|d^4y|$ is the invariant tensor density under time-independent relabellings of the Gaussian rays. The curvature term is retained for inhomogeneous data.

The scalar equations are

$$
V''=-\left(\mathcal R+\frac{16}{L^2}\right)V,
$$

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}Q^2
=\frac34(V')^2+
\left(\mathcal R+\frac{12}{L^2}\right)V^2.
}
\tag{3.4}
$$

The second follows from the Hamiltonian constraint
$\mathcal R+\theta^2-\operatorname{tr}B^2=-12/L^2$.
The momentum equation is
$\nabla_A B^A{}_B-\partial_B\theta=0$.
Equations (3.2)–(3.4) contain no derivative truncation.

The typed extraction is

$$
\mathcal Q:
\{\text{Gaussian Einstein metrics with }\Lambda=-6/L^2\}
\longrightarrow
\Gamma\bigl(\operatorname{End}_0(T\Sigma)\otimes|\Lambda^4T^*\Sigma|\bigr),
\quad g\longmapsto VE\,|d^4y|.
\tag{3.5}
$$

For spatially homogeneous flat slices, $\mathcal R^A{}_B=0$; hence $Q$ is exactly constant and $V''+16V/L^2=0$. This specializes the full equations through the inclusion of homogeneous flat-slice metrics into the domain of (3.5). It is an exact solution sector. The relation of Bianchi-I solutions with cosmological constant to anisotropic Einstein geometries is also discussed by Ren (2016, Appendix A); the five-dimensional spacelike solution used next is verified directly here.

## 4. Solving the finite-amplitude homogeneous sector

The tensor $S$ is expressed in the identified orthonormal transverse frame. Its symmetric endomorphism commutes with the transverse scalar block of $h_*$. Define $\mathcal B,\chi,P$ by (1.2), including $P=\operatorname{diag}(P_w,P_\perp)$. The metric is

The matrix exponential is positive, so every spatial slice is Riemannian. Constants entering the metric amplitude are retained explicitly. At $S=0$, the exact original black-brane interior is recovered, not only its leading Kasner asymptotic.

### Exact Einstein verification

Diagonalize $P$ in the constant spatial frame, with eigenvalues $p_A$. Direct substitution of (1.2) gives

$$
\sum_Ap_A=1,\qquad\sum_Ap_A^2=1.
\tag{4.3}
$$

For $\lambda=cs$, the logarithmic scale-factor derivatives from (4.2) are

$$
H_A=\frac1L\cot\frac{4\lambda}{L}
+\frac4L\left(p_A-\frac14\right)\csc\frac{4\lambda}{L},
\qquad\theta=\sum_AH_A=\frac4L\cot\frac{4\lambda}{L}.
\tag{4.4}
$$

They satisfy

$$
H_A'+\theta H_A=-\frac4{L^2},\qquad
\theta'+\sum_AH_A^2=-\frac4{L^2}.
\tag{4.5}
$$

Thus $R^A{}_B=-4\delta^A{}_B/L^2$, $R_{\lambda\lambda}=4/L^2$, and all mixed components vanish. This proves $\operatorname{Ric}(g_S)=-4g_S/L^2$ for every finite real tracefree symmetric $S$.

Define $v_0=r_h^4/(2L^4)$. The physical volume density and conserved anisotropy are

\boxed{

$$
Q_S=\frac{v_0}{L}

\operatorname{tr}Q_S^2=\frac{12v_0^2}{L^2}\chi^2.
\tag{4.6}
$$

The Hamiltonian (3.4) supplies the factor $\chi$. Fixing the additive input $\mathcal B$ in $Q_S$ and keeping the same reference phase $\vartheta_*$ forces this volume amplitude. No amplitude of $S$ has been removed.

For the future shape operator $K^\sharp=-B$,

$$
P=-\lim_{s\downarrow0}csK^\sharp(s).
\tag{4.7}

Equations (4.6)–(4.7) give the physical interpretation of every output in (1.3).

### The quadratic correction and the infinite-amplitude limit

Let $z=\operatorname{tr}\mathcal B^2$. Expanding the exact formulas, solely to compare coefficients, gives

$$
P_w=-\frac12+\frac12z+O(\|\mathcal B\|^4),
$$

$$

\tag{4.11}

These are the terms absent from the finite substitution $P=P_0+\operatorname{diag}(0,\mathcal B)$. The latter has $\operatorname{tr}P^2=1+z$; (4.11), and exactly (1.2), restore the constraint.

$$
\chi\to\infty,\qquad P_w\to\frac14^{-},

\tag{4.12}
$$

The strain is recovered from a closing inverse denominator and a growing volume amplitude. At fixed $s$, $V_S/V_0\to\infty$. Every finite $S$ still has finite real Kasner exponents. Along sequences with a limiting direction $\mathcal B/\|\mathcal B\|_F$, the transverse exponents also have limits given by (1.2). No directional convergence of a general NS strain field is assumed.

=4\left[

\tag{4.13}

It equals $9/2$ at rest. As $\|S\|_F\to\infty$, its lower limit is at least $9/64$, from the $w$ term alone. Special finite choices can instead approach or realize flat leading Kasner data; no claim that every member has the same nonzero leading coefficient is needed.

At the other endpoint, $s_h=\pi L/(4c)$, the Kasner map is explicitly

At rest its spectrum is $(1,0,0,0)$, the regular background horizon behavior. Therefore this family supplies an exact interior completion of (4.10), not a horizon-regular global completion of the forced boundary flow. The construction that retains spatial dependence and its Einstein constraints follows immediately.

## 5. A compact transverse-traceless lift of the complete velocity

Let

$$
\mathscr U=C^\infty_{c,\operatorname{div}=0}(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3).
$$

A member is a full preterminal velocity snapshot, including its corrections. Introduce one additional spatial coordinate $w$ of length dimension and choose $\ell_w>0$. One explicit choice is

$$
\eta(z)=
\begin{cases}
\exp(1-1/(1-z^2)),&|z|<1,\\
0,&|z|\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad b(w)=w\eta(w/\ell_w).
\tag{5.1}
$$

Then $b(0)=0$, $b'(0)=1$. The scale $\ell_w$ is part of the map's input.

For a velocity $V\in\mathscr U$, define the symmetric four-dimensional tensor

$$
\boxed{
(\mathscr L_bV)_{ij}=b'(w)(S_V)_{ij},\qquad
(\mathscr L_bV)_{wi}=-\frac{b(w)}2\Delta V_i,\qquad
(\mathscr L_bV)_{ww}=0.
}
\tag{5.2}
$$

The source and target, including the structure preserved, are

$$
\mathscr L_b:\mathscr U\longrightarrow
TT_c(\mathbb R^4,\delta),
\qquad
TT_c=\{T=T^T:\operatorname{tr}_\delta T=0,
\ \partial^AT_{AB}=0,\ \operatorname{supp}T\text{ compact}\}.
\tag{5.3}
$$

This is a linear map. Its transverse divergence is

$$
\partial^A(\mathscr L_bV)_{Ai}
=\frac{b'}2\Delta V_i-\frac{b'}2\Delta V_i=0,
$$

and its $w$ divergence is
$-b\Delta(\operatorname{div}V)/2=0$. Trace vanishes by incompressibility. Its complete squared norm is

$$
\boxed{
|\mathscr L_bV|_\delta^2
=b'(w)^2S_V:S_V+\frac{b(w)^2}{2}|\Delta V|^2.
}
\tag{5.4}
$$

The mixed components are the correction required by the four-dimensional momentum constraint. Omitting them would leave the explicit residual $b'\Delta V_i/2$. Equation (5.2) cancels that residual without modifying the input velocity.

### Coordinate-level attachment to the original interior slice

This is the vector-field pushforward under $x\mapsto\xi=dx$. Direct differentiation gives

with time unchanged. Thus the coordinate viscosity is transformed explicitly. The $\nu$ in (2.2), (1.2), and the gravitational calibration below remains the original physical cutoff viscosity; it has not been replaced by $\nu_V$ without the inverse map. The full-field construction uses $V=\mathcal P_dU$ in (5.2).

## 6. Exact nonlinear constraint data at every finite amplitude

Set

$$

\tau_*=-\frac4L\cot\vartheta_*,\qquad
\kappa_* =\frac34\tau_*^2+\frac{12}{L^2}=12a_*^2,

$$
\beta_* =\frac{8a_*\nu}{c^2},\qquad
\bar A_0=-a_*\operatorname{diag}(-3,1,1,1),
\qquad
\bar A(V)=\bar A_0+\beta_*\mathscr L_bV.
\tag{6.1}
$$

Both tensors are flat transverse-traceless. The cross contraction is exactly zero:
$\bar A_0:\mathscr L_bV=-a_*b'\operatorname{tr}S_V=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{

=\kappa_*+\beta_*^2
\left[b'^2S_V:S_V+\frac{b^2}{2}|\Delta V|^2\right]

\tag{6.2}

The coefficient $\beta_*$ is fixed by the preceding finite-radius response, not fitted to the answer. Its alternative expression is

$$
\beta_*

\tag{6.3}
$$

Substituting $r_{\mathrm{ref}}^2=r_h^2\sin(2cs_*/L)$ and (2.2) verifies (6.3) directly.

Solve the scalar equation

$$
\boxed{

\qquad\phi>0,\qquad\phi(Y)\longrightarrow1\quad(|Y|\to\infty).

$$

These powers are those for four spatial dimensions. The general-dimensional conformal decomposition is described by Choquet-Bruhat et al. (2007, §2). For this construction the coefficients are checked from the vacuum constraint with $\Lambda=-6/L^2$ directly, rather than imported from a scalar-potential normalization.

Indeed,

$$
R(h)=-6\phi^{-3}\Delta\phi,\quad
\operatorname{tr}_hK=\tau_*,\quad

$$

Multiplying the Hamiltonian residual by $\phi^3$ yields exactly (6.4). The conformal divergence relation for tracefree tensors is

$$
\nabla_h^A(\phi^{-2}\bar A_{AB})

\tag{6.6}
$$

Therefore the data solve

$$
\boxed{

### Existence and uniqueness of the scalar solution

For each $V\in\mathscr U$, $q-\kappa_*$ is smooth, nonnegative, and compactly supported. No smallness of $V$, $S_V$, or $\Delta V$ is required. Define

$$
M=\left(\frac{\|q\|_\infty}{\kappa_*}\right)^{1/8}.
$$

The constant 1 is a subsolution, since $\kappa_*-q\le0$. The constant $M$ is a supersolution, since $\kappa_*M^8-q\ge0$. On each ball, impose $\phi=1$ at the boundary. On $[1,M]$,

$$
\partial_\phi(\kappa_*\phi^3-q\phi^{-5})
=3\kappa_*\phi^2+5q\phi^{-6}>0.
\tag{6.8}
$$

A monotone construction can be specified explicitly. Choose $C$ not smaller than the supremum of (6.8) on the ball and interval, start $\phi_0=1$, and solve successively

$$
(-6\Delta+C)\phi_{n+1}
=C\phi_n-\kappa_*\phi_n^3+q\phi_n^{-5},
\qquad\phi_{n+1}|_{\partial B_R}=1.
\tag{6.9}
$$

The maximum principle proves $1\le\phi_n\le\phi_{n+1}\le M$. Interior elliptic estimates give convergence to a smooth solution. Uniqueness on a ball follows from (6.8). Exhausting $\mathbb R^4$ by balls and taking a diagonal subsequence gives a smooth bounded entire solution with $1\le\phi\le M$.

To prove the condition at infinity rather than assume it, write $u=\phi-1$ and $F=q-\kappa_*$. Equation (6.4) gives

$$
-6\Delta u+\kappa_*(\phi^3-\phi^{-5})=F\phi^{-5}.
$$

For $\phi\ge1$, $\phi^3-\phi^{-5}\ge3(\phi-1)$. Let

$$
v=(-6\Delta+3\kappa_*)^{-1}F.
$$

The inverse has a positive exponentially decaying Euclidean Green kernel, so $v\ge0$ and $v\to0$. Comparison on every approximating ball gives $0\le u\le v$. Therefore $\phi\to1$. If $\phi_1,\phi_2$ are two positive solutions tending to 1, their difference satisfies $(-6\Delta+a(Y))(\phi_1-\phi_2)=0$, with $a(Y)>0$; the maximum principle proves equality. The same minimum argument shows any positive solution tending to 1 is at least 1.

This proves existence and uniqueness for the complete stated input class. The numerical reference implementation separately tests a manufactured finite-difference problem; it is not needed for this continuum argument.

### A left inverse recovering the entire velocity

The complete velocity, not only its strain, can be recovered from these data. Define the Newton inverse of the Euclidean Laplacian on the decaying class by

$$
(\Delta^{-1}f)(\xi)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R^3}
\frac{f(\eta)}{|\xi-\eta|}\,d^3\eta.
$$
